# Gripper v1.1 – SG90 – Design Notes

**Status:** Built and verified in CAD on 2026-10-14. **Updated the same day for the EduSCARA −35 mm mount-offset rule** (see section 1a). Not yet printed.
**Fusion:** project *Josh Robot Gripper* / folder **Gripper v1.1**. Files: `Gripper v1.1 - SG90` (gripper) and `EduSCARA + Gripper v1.1 (SG90)` (arm scene).
**Local:** `cad/gripper/SG90/`. Contains the `.f3d` and `.step` of the gripper, `STL - printed parts/` (7 parts), `Gripper v1.1 animation.gif` and `arm scene.png`. The arm-scene `.f3d` (11 MB) is kept in Fusion cloud only.
**Derived from:** v1 final (`Gripper v3 - Housing`). The rack and pinion, fingers and rails are unchanged; only the drive and the housing were redone. There is also a sister version with a camera: `v1.1 SG90 + Camera design notes.md`.

## 1. Concept

This is the v1 single-pinion, twin-rack parallel gripper, now driven by a **TowerPro SG90 micro servo** instead of the NEMA 8 placeholder. The housing is rebuilt around the servo's real shape to make the stack as short as possible.

- The SG90's output axis is the pinion axis, so the jaws stay centred on the Z-shaft.
- The pinion drives directly off the servo. The supplied double-arm horn is **trimmed to 13 mm overall** (6.5 mm radius), sits in a pocket in the underside of the pinion, and is held by the servo's own horn screw from the top. The hub collar, hex shaft insert, grub screw and access channel are gone.
- The servo hangs under the base plate by its mounting tabs. Two loose printed **servo spacers** (4 × 7 × 4.86 mm) sit between the tabs and the plate. **M2 × 10 self-tappers** go up through tab → spacer into Ø1.6 pilots in the base plate. (Print prep, 2026-10-14: the posts used to be part of the base plate, screwed from the top into nuts.)
- The motor adapter and the four separate standoffs are replaced by one printed **Arm Frame**: the EduSCARA plate plus four integral columns. The base plate screws onto the columns with M3 screws from the top.

## 1a. Mounting requirement: −35 mm offset (2026-10-14, applies to all grippers)

**Rule:** the pincer focal point (the jaw centreline, where the fingers meet) must sit **35 mm closer to the robot than the mounting point**. That means 35 mm back along link2 toward the elbow (arm +X in the saved pose), at the same height.

**How v1.1 meets it:**
- The racks, pinion, servo and base plate are unchanged and stay centred on the pinion axis.
- The **Arm Frame plate** is extended to x ±25, y −47…+22.
- The Ø18.2 × 3 socket, 4 × M2 on Ø14 and the Ø3.4 centre hole move to **(0, −35)** (parameter `mount_offset` = 35 mm). Gripper +y equals arm +X, so the jaws end up 35 mm toward the elbow.
- The arm M2 heads no longer sit under the servo, so the counterbores were removed and the plate thinned from 6 to **5 mm** (3 mm socket, 2 mm floor). The arm face to fingertip distance drops from 67.7 to **66.7 mm**.
- The gripper now sits partly under the end of link2. A **notch** (x ±18.5 for y ≥ 13.5; parameters `l2_notch_half_x`, `l2_notch_y`) clears link2_0 and its two M2 button heads, which hang down to z 37 / 35.7 beyond arm x −50.
- The four frame columns are outside the notch and unchanged. The mount overhangs the −y columns by about 25 mm (a 5 mm PLA plate carrying about 60 g).
- **Arm scene clearance (saved pose):** link2_0 1.5 mm, link2 M2 heads 1.75 mm, link2_1 2.5 mm. This is about the same gap the stock gripper's top plate has under link2.
- **Risk:** if the Z-shaft can retract higher than the saved pose, these gaps are the limit. Confirm the top of Z travel on the real arm.
- **Fingertip centre on the arm:** (−65, −125, −26.7) in the saved pose, against (−100, −125) for the shaft.

## 3. Physical pros

1. **14 mm shorter** than v1, and 0.3 mm shorter than the stock gripper. That gives back Z travel and cuts the moment on the arm's Z axis.
2. **About half the mass** (about 57 g against 110 g), which helps the hobby-servo arm.
3. **Much more force** (about 8.8 N stall against about 1 N) with no gearbox, because the SG90 already contains roughly a 300:1 reduction.
4. **Plain PWM control** on one signal wire, as the stock gripper already does. No stepper driver, no homing, absolute position on power-up.
5. **Fewer parts and no grub-screw coupling.** The horn-in-pocket drive removes v1's weakest link (the grub screw in a printed hex insert).
6. **Same kinematics as v1**: exact straight-line, level jaws, self-centring, fingers close to zero gap.
7. **Parametric**: every sketch fully constrained. Changing a servo dimension moves the posts, holes, columns and arm plate with it.

## 4. Physical cons and risks

1. **Coarse, play-prone position control.** The roughly 1° deadband plus gear-train play (about 1–2° on an SG90) gives about ±0.2–0.35 mm of jaw uncertainty. That is worse than the NEMA 8 at 1/16 microstepping (0.02 mm). The pot-based feedback also drifts with temperature and supply voltage.
2. **Too much force for small parts.** Stall force can crush a 1/8 W resistor (Ø1.8 mm) or strip the module 1 printed teeth. Always command a position only slightly past contact, and calibrate the angle for each resistor size.
3. **Servo jitter and heat when holding.** A servo holding against a part draws stall-ish current (about 0.5–0.7 A at 5 V) and can buzz. Don't leave it squeezing for long.
4. **Horn must be trimmed** to 13 mm overall to fit inside the pinion root circle. The horn arms carry the full torque through a 1.4 mm plastic section. Alternatives: print the 21-tooth spline into the pinion, or use a metal disc horn.
5. **Servo offset.** The case extends 16.4 mm to one side of the axis, so the mass sits slightly off-centre (about 5 mm). Negligible for the arm, but the servo cable needs routing out of the open frame side.
6. **Assembly order.** The Arm Frame goes onto the arm first (the base plate and camera cradle cover its M2 screws). The servo is screwed to the base plate from below before the base plate goes onto the columns. Full sequence in `v1.1 print and assembly guide.md`.
7. **Arm Frame is a solid 5 mm plate** with a 35 mm cantilevered mount (18.3 cm³, about 23 g, with Ø7 columns). This is an obvious place to lighten. A rib between the −y columns and the socket would stiffen the overhang.
9. **Tight clearance under link2** (1.5–2.5 mm in the saved pose) because of the −35 mm offset. It depends on the real Z travel limit.
8. **Not modelled:** the servo cable, the horn screw head (1.3 mm proud of the pinion top, which clears in CAD by inspection), and the tab nuts.

## 5. Verification performed (CAD)

- **Interference:** no clashes at 0/25/50/75/100 % of the stroke, across all bodies including the SG90 and horn. The servo tabs sit on the spacers with zero gap, as intended. The servo is 0.3 mm from the Arm Frame and 1.26 mm below the pinion; the horn has 0.3 mm clearance in its pocket.
- **Printability (2026-10-14):** overhang analysis per part, print-oriented STLs checked watertight with volumes within 0.1 % of the CAD. Only the racks need support (under the jaw bridge). See the print guide.
- **Arm scene (after the offset):** no clash with any EduSCARA part. The arm face is flush with the Z-shaft face at z = 40, with the socket on the shaft axis. Nearest parts: link2_0 1.5 mm, link2 M2 heads 1.75 mm, link2_1 2.5 mm.
- **Constraints and timeline:** all sketches fully constrained, no unhealthy features.
- **Not verified:** real SG90 tolerances (they vary between clones by about ±0.3 mm, so measure yours and edit `servo_*`), horn trim fit, print accuracy, real grip force, servo backlash.
