# Gripper v1.1 – Print & Assembly Guide

**Applies to:** `Gripper v1.1 - SG90` and `Gripper v1.1 - SG90 + Camera` (one shared print set; the cradle is for the camera version only).
**Files (this folder, `cad/gripper/Print/`):**
- `Gripper v1.1 print plate.3mf`: every part, plus the fit tests already in print orientation on one plate (150 × 96 mm). Open it in Bambu Studio or PrusaSlicer.
- `STL (print-oriented)/`: one STL per part, rotated for printing and sitting on z = 0.
- `Gripper v1.1 - Print Plate.f3d`: the Fusion layout (cloud: *Gripper v1.1 / Gripper v1.1 - Print Plate*).
- `print plate.png`: a preview of the plate.

All STLs were checked: watertight, volumes within 0.1 % of the CAD, every part on z = 0.

## 1. Print the fit tests first (about 10 minutes)

| Coupon | Checks | Pass if |
|---|---|---|
| **00a Fit test A** (rail + holes) | 2.6 × 1.3 mm rail (same as the base plate rails). Holes: Ø2.4 (M2 clearance), Ø1.6 (M2 self-tap pilot), Ø2.6 (M3 self-tap pilot). | An M2 screw drops through the Ø2.4 hole, an M2 self-tapper bites firmly into Ø1.6, and an M3 self-tapper cuts into Ø2.6 without splitting. |
| **00b Fit test B** (groove slider + horn pocket) | 3 × 1.5 mm groove (same as the rack underside), the pinion's horn pocket, the Ø2.4 screw hole. | B slides along A's rail smoothly with no rocking (≤ 0.1–0.2 mm play). The trimmed SG90 horn (see §4) drops into the pocket with no force and no rattle. |

**If the fits are tight:** in the slicer, in Bambu Studio set *X-Y hole compensation* to +0.05–0.1 mm. PrusaSlicer has no hole-only compensation, so ease the holes with a Ø2.4 / Ø1.6 / Ø2.6 drill instead. If the rail is tight in the groove, sand the rail tops lightly. **If loose:** that's OK for the horn pocket (screw clamping takes it up). For the rail, a loose fit means more jaw wobble.

## 2. Slicer settings (PLA, 0.4 mm nozzle)

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| Layer height | **0.16 mm** for the whole plate (0.12 mm on the pinion and racks if you set it per object, for smoother teeth) |
| Walls / top / bottom | 4 walls (1.6 mm), 5 top, 4 bottom layers |
| Infill | 40 % gyroid (pinion: 100 %, it's tiny) |
| Elephant-foot compensation | **On, 0.10–0.15 mm**. The pinion teeth and the rack undersides sit on the bed, and a flared first layer makes gears bind. |
| Brim | 5 mm on the two **racks**, the **cradle** and the two **spacers** (tall or tiny footprints). Off for the rest. |
| Supports | **Only under the racks' jaw bridge** (see §3). Use tree supports, build plate only, and paint them on (support enforcer). Turn off automatic supports elsewhere, or the Arm Frame socket gets filled with support. |
| Seam | Aligned, on the back. Keep it off the rack teeth if you can. |
| Temperature / cooling | Normal PLA profile, part cooling on |
| Estimate | About 46 cm³ solid, roughly **42 g of PLA** and **3–4 h** at 0.16 mm on a Bambu/Prusa (your slicer will give exact numbers) |

**Trim the horn:** cut the double-arm horn so its overall length is **13 mm** (6.5 mm from the centre each side), and round the cut ends slightly. Check it in fit test B.

## 4a. Self-tapping screws into PLA: how to make them last

All the screws that go into plastic (servo tabs, base plate → columns, cradle) are self-tapping into pilot holes sized at about 80–85 % of the screw diameter (Ø1.6 for M2, Ø2.6 for M3), with at least about 1.5–2.2 mm of plastic around each thread.
- **Use screws made for plastic** (PT / "plastite" / "for plastic" pan-head; the SG90's own screws are this type). Not machine screws: their fine threads strip PLA.
- **First assembly:** drive slowly by hand and stop at snug. The first pass cuts the thread. Never use a power driver on full speed.
- **Re-assembly:** turn the screw backwards until you feel it drop into the existing thread, then tighten. That avoids cutting a new thread, so a hole survives about 5–10 re-assemblies.
- **If a hole strips:** use the next length up, or a drop of thin CA glue in the hole, let it set and re-drive. Or drill it to Ø4.0 and fit an M3 heat-set insert (the Ø7 columns have room for a short 3–4 mm one).
- PLA creeps slightly under constant clamp, so re-check tightness after the first few hours of use.
- Test your actual screws in the fit-test coupon A pilots (Ø1.6 and Ø2.6) before the main print.

## 5. Assembly order

1. **Clean up the prints.** Remove the rack bridge supports, deburr the rack grooves and base plate rails, and check each rack slides along its rail with only light play. Take off any first-layer flare on the pinion teeth.
2. **Centre the servo before fitting the pinion.** Power the SG90 and command **90°** (centre). The pinion turns **71.6°** from fully open to fully closed, so centring the servo with the jaws **half open (12.5 mm gap)** gives about **54° = open** and **126° = closed**. Check the direction on the bench and swap the two values if it's mirrored.
3. **Fit the Arm Frame to the arm:** socket over the Z-shaft spigot, 4 × M2 × 6 through the floor. Do this first; the base plate and cradle cover these screws later.
4. **Fit the servo to the base plate.** Push the gear tower up through the Ø13 hole, put a **spacer** between each tab and the plate underside, and drive **M2 × 10** up through tab → spacer → base plate pilot. Snug, not tight.
5. **Fit the horn and pinion.** With the servo still at 90°, press the trimmed horn onto the spline (arms along the rack direction). Drop the pinion over it (pocket down) and fix it with the horn screw through the pinion top.
6. **Fit the racks.** Slide each rack onto its rail from the ends so both jaws are at the half-open position (fingers 12.5 mm apart, symmetric about the pinion) as the teeth engage. Sweep the servo through 54°–126° and check both jaws move evenly with no binding.
7. **Camera version only:** slide the XIAO into the cradle (lens down, USB-C end last; see the camera design notes), then screw the cradle to the base plate underside with **2 × M2 × 12** from below.
8. **Mount the base plate assembly onto the Arm Frame columns** with **4 × M3 × 20** from the top, through the guide-wall corners.
9. **Wire it up:** SG90 signal / 5 V / GND (keep 5 V capable of about 0.7 A stall), plus camera USB-C. Route the cables out of the open frame side and tie them to a column.
10. **Final check:** command 54° → 126° → 54° slowly. The jaws should close to touching and open to 25 mm. Never command past the closed angle; the SG90 can push about 8.8 N per jaw at stall.

## 6. What changed to make it printable (2026-10-14)

- **Servo posts are now loose printed spacers.** The base plate prints flat with no supports.
- **Servo-tab screws go in from below** into Ø1.6 pilots, and the tower hole shrank from Ø15 to Ø13. The old top counterbore for the near-end screw overlapped the tower hole, so its head would only have been half supported.
- **FDM clearances:** horn pocket clearance 0.2 → 0.3 mm; the pinion's horn-screw hole Ø2.2 → Ø2.4.
- **Frame columns Ø5.5 → Ø7** (2026-10-15), so the M3 self-tappers have about 2.2 mm of wall instead of 1.4 mm and won't split the columns.
- **Camera cradle insertion slot**, so the camera module can slide in.
- Interference re-checked at 0/50/100 % stroke after every change: none.
