## 1. Concept: vertical "look, then move" camera on the Z-shaft axis

- A **Seeed XIAO ESP32S3 Sense** (ESP32-S3 board, Sense expansion board and OV2640/OV3660 camera) lies **flat** in a printed **Camera Cradle** with the lens pointing **straight down** (optical axis vertical, `cam_tilt` = 0°).
- The camera can't sit on the jaw centreline, because the pinion, horn and servo are there. Instead it sits on the **−y side of the gripper at y = −35 mm** (`cam_lens_y = −mount_offset`). Because of the −35 mm mount offset, that puts the **camera axis on the EduSCARA Z-shaft axis**, directly under the shaft.
- **Workflow:** the arm positions the *camera* over the work area and takes a clean top-down image. It locates the resistor, then moves 35 mm along link2 (outward, away from the elbow) so the *jaw centre* lands where the camera was looking. The camera-to-jaw offset is exactly the mount offset.
- Because the camera is on the Z-shaft axis, rotating the end effector about Z (zrot) spins the image about its own centre; the camera centre doesn't move. That makes calibration simple.
- The cradle sits in the height band between the base plate and the Arm Frame (z −18.8…−5), so **the gripper is no taller** than the plain SG90 version (66.7 mm). It also stays within the Arm Frame footprint, so the gripper is no wider.

## 2a. Inserting the camera

1. Hold the XIAO Sense flat with the lens facing down (toward the fingers) and the USB-C end toward you.
2. Push it into the open end of the cradle, the end facing the arm's ±Y side (gripper +x). The camera module rides in the insertion slot and the board slides on the ledges.
3. Slide it all the way in until it stops against the closed end wall. The lens then sits over the window, on the Z-shaft axis.
4. Plug in USB-C at the open end. This works with the gripper on or off the arm, because nothing blocks the open end. The slide-in path was swept in CAD over 0–39 mm with the jaws open and closed: no clashes.
5. **Retention:** none yet beyond friction (0.2 mm clearance). A drop of hot glue or a strip of Kapton tape holds it for now. A screw-on end tab (1 × M2) is the planned fix.

## 3. Physical pros

1. **True top-down image.** It's orthographic-like, so a single scale factor plus lens distortion calibrates it. That's much simpler than the earlier 40.6° oblique view.
2. **Camera on the Z axis.** End-effector rotation doesn't move the image centre, and the camera-to-jaw offset is a fixed 35 mm along link2, the same vector as the mount offset.
3. **No height or footprint penalty.** It fits under the Arm Frame, beside the base plate.
4. **Unobstructed view.** Nothing on the gripper enters a ±33° cone.
5. **One extra part and two screws.** The board slides in and out for programming, and USB-C stays accessible.
6. **On-board compute and Wi-Fi.** The XIAO can stream images and could also generate the SG90's PWM.

## 4. Physical cons and risks

1. **It doesn't see the grasp.** The jaws are 35 mm away, outside the view. Grasp success depends on the arm's repeatability over the 35 mm move. Verify picks by re-imaging the spot after the pick, or with another sensor.
2. **Extra arm motion per pick** (image, move 35 mm, descend). This adds a little cycle time, and the vision-to-jaw offset needs one-time calibration (camera-to-shaft-axis offset and image rotation).
3. **Short working distance.** The lens is only about 37–42 mm from the table at pick height. The OV2640's fixed focus may need refocusing (the lens unscrews), and the field of view is small. Image from higher Z for a wider view, then descend.
4. **The lens position is assumed.** The Sense camera position on the expansion board isn't in the datasheet. If it's off-centre, the window (11 mm square) allows about ±1.2 mm. Measure the real board and edit `cam_lens_y/z`, or just calibrate it out.
5. **The lens sticks out 3.4 mm below the cradle face** with no guard. It's 33 mm above the fingertips, so it's unlikely to hit anything, but it isn't protected.
6. **Assembly order.** The cradle sits under the arm M2 screws, so the Arm Frame must be screwed to the arm **before** the base plate and cradle go on (the normal order anyway).
7. **Heat and EMI.** The ESP32-S3 with the camera running gets warm next to PLA, and the servo's noisy supply needs decoupling. No cable routing or strain relief is modelled.
8. **The cradle position is computed by script** (a move feature). After changing `cam_lens_y/z` or `cam_tilt`, the cradle and web need regenerating.

## 5. Verification performed (CAD)

- **Interference:** none at 0/50/100 % stroke, across all bodies including the XIAO, cradle, SG90 and horn.
- **Insertion:** the XIAO model swept out along +x from 0 to 39 mm, in 3 mm steps, with the jaws open and closed: zero intersection with any gripper body. Before the slot was added, the camera module jammed against the cradle's front wall.
- **Field of view:** 27° and 33° half-angle cones from the lens to z 45 intersect no gripper body at 0/50/100 % stroke.
- **Arm scene:** no clash with the EduSCARA. The camera sits at arm x −108.9…−91.1, y −135.5…−113.3, centred under the Z-shaft. Other clearances are as in the SG90 version (link2_0 1.5 mm, link2 M2 heads 1.75 mm, link2_1 2.5 mm).
- **Constraints:** all sketches fully constrained, no unhealthy features.
- **Not verified:** the real camera position, field of view and focus at about 40 mm, cradle stiffness, thermals.

## 6. Open items

- Measure the real XIAO Sense (lens offset, stack height) and update the parameters.
- Check focus at 37–60 mm; refocus if needed.
- Calibration routine: camera intrinsics, mm-per-pixel at the imaging height, and the camera-to-jaw vector (nominally 35 mm along link2) plus image rotation relative to the arm.
- Cable clips for USB-C and servo leads; consider powering the servo from the XIAO's 5 V.
- Optional lens guard lip.
- Retention for the slide-in board: a screw-on end tab (1 × M2) beside the USB-C plug.

## 7. History

- First camera version (2026-10-14): cradle tilted 40.6°, aimed at the fingertip midpoint from 30 mm off-centre. It saw into the jaw gap but needed oblique calibration, and it was hidden by the fingers when they closed.
- Replaced the same day with the vertical, on-shaft-axis camera (this version) at Josh's request. An on-axis camera looking through a hollow pinion with an offset servo (option B) was considered and not chosen.

## 8. Comparison context

See section 7 of `v1.1 SG90 design notes.md`. In short: same height (66.7 mm), footprint (50 × 69), force and mount offset as the plain SG90 version. It's about 13 g heavier and adds a vertical camera on the Z-shaft axis for look-then-move picking.
